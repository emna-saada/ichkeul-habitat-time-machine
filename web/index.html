Research feature · Earth observation · Wetland ecology

The lake follows the rain. The marshes don't.

Twenty seasonal habitat maps (2017–2026) of Ichkeul National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage wetland in northern Tunisia, built from Sentinel-2 imagery with machine-learning and deep segmentation models. Open water rises and falls with rainfall. Summer marshes are shrinking, and rainfall does not explain it.

Emna SaadaGeoinformaticsData: Sentinel-2 · Landsat · PERSIANN-CDR

Key findings

    01

    The site

    The last lake of a chain that once crossed North Africa

    Ichkeul is a shallow, brackish lake with seasonal marshes and a wooded limestone massif, 25 km from Bizerte. It was one of the four most important wintering sites for waterbirds in the western Mediterranean. Since the 1980s, upstream dams have cut its freshwater supply.

    Lake Ichkeul
    ~89 km²

    Shallow (~1 m) and brackish; Potamogeton beds feed wintering waterfowl.

    Garaet Ichkeul marshes
    27–30 km²

    Temporarily flooded reedbeds and sedges, increasingly halophytic.

    Djebel Ichkeul
    13.6 km²

    Wooded massif rising 511 m from the plain: the stable reference class.

    Catchment
    2,080 km²

    Eight wadis; dams now control ~40 % of the catchment and ~70 % of inflow.

    03

    The breathing lake

    How often is each pixel under water?

    The share of the 20 seasonal maps in which a pixel is open water. The dark core is the permanent lake. The pale fringe is the seasonal flood zone, the marshes the lake reclaims each winter.

    04

    Ten years of change

    What the lake and marshes lost

    Class areas inside the basin envelope for every season, with Mann–Kendall trend tests and Sen's slopes. With ten points per seasonal series, only strong, monotonic changes reach significance.

    05

    Attribution

    Rain, or something else?

    Open-water area is set against satellite rainfall over the catchment (PERSIANN-CDR, 2015–2026). If rain explains the swings, a time term should add little. A residual trend would point to upstream water capture and management.

    06

    Forty years of water

    From the dams to today

    Seasonal lake water area from Landsat 5, 7 and 8 (1984–2021), continued with the Sentinel-2 maps (2017–2026). The five overlapping years check that the two sensors agree.

    07

    Model evaluation

    Which models map habitat best with few labels?

    Every model is scored on the same spatially held-out test chips, with the same label fractions. Scores are against weak labels on 12 test locations × 20 seasons. Hand-labelled gold points come next.

    08

    Method

    From raw scenes to habitat maps

    The pipeline is reproducible end to end from open data: make data → labels → baselines → gpu → analysis → web.

    1. Composites

      Sentinel-2 L2A, SCL cloud mask with 60 m dilation, wet and dry medians per year, 10 bands.

    2. Weak labels

      Agreement of WorldCover 2020 and 2021, JRC water occurrence, DEM and seasonal NDVI/MNDWI rules.

    3. Chips & split

      224 px chips; 80 locations split 70/15/15 by spatial block, stratified by wetland share.

    4. Models

      RF on indices, RF on AlphaEarth, U-Net; Prithvi-EO-2.0 and TerraMind on GPU.

    5. Maps & change

      20 seasonal maps, areas, Mann–Kendall/Sen, transitions, inundation frequency.

    6. Attribution

      Rainfall regression with unique R², gradient boosting with SHAP, Landsat 1984–2021.
