01 · The site

The last lake of a chain that once crossed North Africa

Ichkeul is a shallow, brackish lake with seasonal marshes and a wooded limestone massif, 25 km from Bizerte. It was one of the four most important wintering sites for waterbirds in the western Mediterranean. Since the 1980s, upstream dams have cut its freshwater supply.

Lake Ichkeul
~89 km²

Shallow (~1 m), brackish; Potamogeton beds feed wintering waterfowl.

Garaet Ichkeul marshes
27–30 km²

Temporarily flooded reedbeds and sedges, increasingly halophytic.

Djebel Ichkeul
13.6 km²

Isolated wooded massif rising 511 m from the plain: the stable reference class.

Catchment
2,080 km²

Eight wadis; dams now control ~40 % of the catchment and ~70 % of inflow.

A double seasonal pulse

In winter the rivers fill the lake. It spreads ~30 km² over the marshes, freshens, and drains to the Bizerte lagoon through the Oued Tinja. In summer evaporation lowers it, the flow reverses and seawater enters. Salinity peaks July–October.

So a February image is never compared with a July one: every analysis runs on two seasonal series, wet (Jan–Mar) and dry (Jul–Sep).

08 · Method

From raw scenes to habitat maps

The pipeline is reproducible end to end from open data: make data → make labels → make baselines → make gpu → make analysis → make web.

Composites

Sentinel-2 L2A COGs, SCL cloud mask (60 m dilation), wet/dry medians per year, 10 bands.

Weak labels

Agreement of WorldCover 2020+2021, JRC water occurrence, DEM and seasonal NDVI/MNDWI rules.

Chips & split

224 px chips; 80 locations split 70/15/15 by spatial block, stratified by wetland share.

Models

RF (indices), RF (AlphaEarth), U-Net, Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M, TerraMind-base.

Maps & change

20 seasonal maps, areas, Mann–Kendall/Sen, transitions, inundation frequency.

Attribution

Rainfall regression, unique R², gradient boosting + SHAP; Landsat 1984–2021.
